# 4 Synthesis of Technical Progress and Research Directions

## 4.1 Solution mechanisms and scalability

The computational burden of large-constellation scheduling arises from candidate satellite–task assignments, conflicts among shared activities, and exchange of states and plans between participants. The methods reviewed in Section 3 address different coupling structures and thus offer different mechanisms of scalability. Branch-and-price [33](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cor.2025.107259) separates public/exclusive task and battery decisions into master and pricing structures while retaining bounds for the specified discrete model. Beam–power decomposition [74](https://doi.org/10.23919/JCC.ja.2022-0864) separates illumination from continuous power control. Both exploit mathematical structure, but their subproblem variables, computational burden, and coordination conditions depend on their respective applications. Their potential transfer across service domains lies in the decomposition principle, not in unconditional reuse of their models or observed numerical gains.

Local coordination and reuse of existing plans also reduce computation, but entail choices involving schedule quality and coordination scope. In continuing-planning experiments, Parjan and Chien [34](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.I011215) find that incremental modification is faster than rebuilding a schedule while generating more repeated observations. In geometric-neighborhood experiments, Zilberstein et al. [35](https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.1.16997) reduce the maximum agent computation and messages in large test cases but retain gaps in request satisfaction relative to comparison methods. The first approach exploits temporal persistence of plans; the second exploits the locality of task conflicts. Ground-assisted routing [79](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comcom.2022.02.015) similarly organizes forwarding state to reduce updates, though its full performance evidence remains to be checked. Dynamic laser-link routing [121](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAES.2024.3415571) demonstrates in its cited author version that the benefit of route persistence depends on link establishment delay. Plan reuse and route persistence both exploit previously accepted decisions, with different risks of duplicate use, lost opportunities, and action costs.

**Table 8. Technical mechanisms, direct effects, and trade-offs**

| Mechanism | What is altered | Direct benefit or purpose | Associated cost and conditions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mathematical programming and structured decomposition | Variable representation, master/subproblem separation | Exploits structure; some formulations retain bounds | Pricing/subproblem burden, discretization and formulation constraints |
| Hierarchical planning and local repair | Global assignment, local ordering and revisions | Restricts each solve and preserves usable activities | Interface consistency, existing commitments, duplicate or omitted tasks |
| Distributed coordination | Data collection, conflict resolution, decision authority | Reduces some centralized burden and permits local reactions | Connectivity, stale information, conflict density affect result and latency |
| Prediction and learning-assisted search | Demand forecasts, initial solutions, neighborhoods | Provides prospective load knowledge or search guidance | Prediction error, training, preprocessing, and downstream repair |
| Learning policies with safety constraints | Online actions and runtime checks | Shifts part of computation offline, enables fast responses | Training coverage, action masking, communications, fallback |
| Multiresource and cross-function joint decisions | Tasks, routes, capacities, execution states | Makes downstream bottlenecks affect current choices | Added variables, computation and information requirements |

Scalability thus concerns computing location, timing, and solution quality together. Equal satellite counts do not imply equal difficulty when candidate windows, task conflicts, and network links differ. Claims of improvement through decomposition, neighborhoods, or incremental updates must be tied to the exact formulation, baseline, workload, and metric under evaluation.

## 4.2 Resource coupling from local activities to delivered services

How a model represents downstream resources determines whether they affect earlier decisions. Joint observation–transmission scheduling [42](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMC.2021.3076088) lets downlink windows influence acquisition; energy-constrained scheduling [43](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2025.02.008) lets charging influence imaging and transmission feasibility. In staged observation–processing work [53](https://doi.org/10.1002/sat.70082), an acquisition schedule determines downstream workload, whereas contact-graph scheduling [106](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adhoc.2023.103289) selects acquisition and delivery paths together on a per-request basis. These methods link different stages: some constrain current selection by later resources, others optimize downstream decisions given an upstream plan, and still others select a delivery-feasible path. Their decision sets and definitions of mission completion are not interchangeable.

In communication networks, resource coupling occurs within continuous traffic flows. Single-satellite beam/power control [74](https://doi.org/10.23919/JCC.ja.2022-0864) alters supply–demand matching through power allocation; NGSO/GSO coexistence and multisatellite association [75](https://doi.org/10.1109/LCOMM.2022.3213912) introduce load balance and specified co-channel-interference considerations, though the exact model is not yet verified beyond the publisher abstract. Demand-aware allocation [76](https://doi.org/10.1109/TWC.2025.3545745) shows a load-dependent difference between overall strategies in the author-version tests. Path delay enters handover evaluation [77](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cja.2024.09.039), while ground-relayed segment routing [80](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcan.2022.09.022) handles traffic splitting and fault recovery subject to link capacity. Establishing a laser link adds another route-update cost [121](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAES.2024.3415571). The significance of coupling therefore depends on the bottleneck: illumination, access, and instantaneous shortest paths each represent only part of the service process, while power, future path capacity, and the time needed for configuration determine realized service.

We compare coupling by the service outcome to which a model connects its decisions. An observation task may end at acquisition, inference, or delivery; a communication network provides throughput, latency, availability, and fairness over time. A joint choice includes later feasibility in the present selection, a sequential model passes an upstream decision to a downstream allocation, and feedback triggers a revised decision after state updates. Contact-graph studies show that increased delivery count may bring slower deliveries into the *successful* sample, changing the interpretation of mean conditional latency. Beam-allocation benefits likewise change with traffic load. Service completion, sample definition, and computation/coordination cost must therefore be considered together when claiming benefits from more coupled resource models.

Maintenance and safe execution alter business opportunities by changing asset availability over time. Inventory models [18](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.A34387) represent spare capacity, and integrated servicing policies [23](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.A36684) expand recovery options. Maneuvers, faults, and repair actions can temporarily change imaging opportunities or network capacities. The shared operational interface is the capability actually available after specific events and delays. Owning a spare asset, dispatching a servicer, and restoring normal service are distinct stages. Representing them explicitly where necessary can reveal how maintenance investment affects lost services.

## 4.3 Information conditions for distributed and learning methods

Studies of distributed observation show that the meaning of shared information changes an agent's ability to recognize competition. Parjan and Chien [34](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.I011215) compare sharing request satisfaction and competitor-related information, thereby giving members different knowledge about existing allocations. Geometric-neighborhood methods [35](https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.1.16997) coordinate locally when public requests and constellation geometry are known but other agents' full capabilities are not. Sharing scope determines both message burden and which competitive interactions an agent can account for.

A record of a completed task and an intention to perform a future task are different coordination inputs. Herrmann et al. [65](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.A35736) reuse single-satellite policies across members and communicate completed imaging/downlink states. Even with unrestricted communication in their model, members may select the same target in one decision interval before intended actions have been confirmed. Connectivity determines whether messages can travel, while their content determines which concurrent conflicts can be anticipated. Policy reuse reduces the burden of joint training, but coordination quality is limited by available shared states.

Message arrival time further changes usefulness. Communication-limited observation scheduling [38](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.asr.2020.02.018) predicts communication contacts; mission reward and message cost vary with sync/async choices. Generic lossy-network bundle experiments [118](https://doi.org/10.1109/SAM.2018.8448984) provide a separate reference for delayed or lost exchanges but do not establish actual satellite-link failure rates. Hierarchical communication management [81](https://doi.org/10.3390/s25030902) exchanges global configurations, local needs, and prices, while dynamic laser-link routing [121](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAES.2024.3415571) assumes advance computation and dissemination of paths. Both require relevant information before local actions can take effect. Task allocations, bids, service loads, and link-activity states consequently have different update rules, so coordination depends jointly on information scope and timeliness.

Learning has multiple roles in these settings. Demand prediction [73](https://doi.org/10.3390/aerospace11030191) produces load estimates; learning-assisted large-neighborhood search [63](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2025.128502) supplies an initial schedule that is refined by combinatorial search; a reusable policy [65](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.A35736) produces direct member choices; and a shielded controller [96](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40295-026-00631-w) combines action generation and safety restrictions. These contribute to services through downstream search, coordination, or runtime checks. Training-time global information and execution-time local states also differ. The value of combining learning with distributed decisions depends on whether the available local observations are sufficient for resource competition and safe action.

## 4.4 Operational evidence from commercial services and flight experiments

Public operational materials supply concrete meanings for service completion, resource commitment, and recovery. Planet's Tasking API [51](https://docs.planet.com/develop/apis/tasking/) separates acquisition, quality acceptance, and delivery; further attempts for selected products mean that an acquisition need not complete the customer request. AWS Ground Station [52](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ground-station/latest/ug/contacts.html) records a configuration snapshot when a contact is scheduled, and changes to the reservation's time or mission profile generally require cancellation and rescheduling. The separate AWS contact-versioning interface also supports certain target or ephemeris updates without changing the reserved time. These interfaces illustrate different stages of completion and the conditions under which accepted resource allocations may be revised. Flight tests connect state feedback to actions and later choices. IPEX [111](https://ai.jpl.nasa.gov/public/projects/ipex/) ties single-spacecraft processing, resources, and ongoing planning. Starling [114](https://www.nasa.gov/smallspacecraft/what-is-starling/) demonstrates observation coordination among four members. Starling 1.5 [115](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/ames/nasa-starling-and-spacex-starlink-improve-space-traffic-coordination/) demonstrates coordination of a real maneuver under a **simulated conjunction**. Starlink's disclosed process [89](https://space-safety.starlink.com/docs/space-safety-articles/starlink_cola_conops/) and ADRAS-J [97](https://www.astroscale.com/en/news/astroscales-adras-j-achieves-historic-15-meter-approach-to-space-debris) provide different evidence of repeated risk assessment and safe abort. The scale, tasks, authorities, and observed outcomes differ among these sources; they cannot be combined into one claimed end-to-end flight demonstration.

Software work examines configuration and state properties. Supervised reconfiguration [119](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10270-026-01426-1) concerns a satellite's real-time software process scheduler, not distributed observation allocation. Model checking of operational designs [120](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10270-025-01281-6) addresses behavior under formal specifications and scaling costs. Such evidence concerns implementation and verification objects, not directly measured user-service benefit. Overall, operational sources contribute definitions of completion and commitment, records of actions triggered by states, and evidence of responsibility shared across organizations. Their operational meaning is essential when interpreting what mathematical models can support, but service gains remain specific to the actual task and test.

## 4.5 Research challenges and future directions

**Connecting capability restoration to lost services.** Inventory policies, dual supply channels, and integrated servicing expand maintenance options [18](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.A34387), [19](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.A36281), [23](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.A36684). Yet inventory demand satisfaction, spare arrival, commissioning, and restored service are distinct states. How spare locations, transfer waits, and operational reallocation interact to determine outage duration is a concrete connection between maintenance planning and service loss. Evaluating identical maintenance investments against common failure and demand sequences can quantify the effect of representing or omitting these transitions.

**Selecting an appropriate scope of end-to-end joint decisions.** Contact-graph scheduling [106](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adhoc.2023.103289) jointly selects acquisition and delivery routes, whereas staged acquisition–processing [53](https://doi.org/10.1002/sat.70082) allocates downstream processors from a given acquisition plan. Demand-aware beams [76](https://doi.org/10.1109/TWC.2025.3545745), path-aware handover [77](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cja.2024.09.039), and dynamic laser-link routing [121](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAES.2024.3415571) incorporate load, downstream path cost, and link establishment in distinct ways. The appropriate coupling scope depends on whether downstream conditions materially change current choices and whether sequential inputs or feedback can capture that influence. Comparing joint, staged, and reactive options under common demands and disruptions can reveal effects on delivery, delay, computing cost, and coordination.

**Online updates with delayed information and accepted commitments.** Incremental planning [34](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.I011215), contact-aware bundles [38](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.asr.2020.02.018), and hierarchical resource management [81](https://doi.org/10.3390/s25030902) exhibit different dependencies on retained assignments, communication timing, and local authority. AWS contact reservations [52](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ground-station/latest/ug/contacts.html) impose conditions on the modification of committed ground resources, while laser-link establishment [121](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAES.2024.3415571) makes changing a routing plan itself costly. Identifying when local repair, global replanning, and activation of a new plan are permitted is therefore an operational scheduling problem. Describing state exchange, recomputation, configuration, and executed outcomes on one time axis allows service losses to be compared.

**Tracing learning gains to the operational outcome.** Traffic predictors [73](https://doi.org/10.3390/aerospace11030191), DRL-produced initial schedules followed by search [63](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2025.128502), and shielded controllers [96](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40295-026-00631-w) improve different components. Prediction accuracy, candidate-solution quality, and instantaneous policy returns can lead to different service results once resource competition, communication, and constraint checks intervene. Evaluating training costs, downstream repair, and fallback by module responsibility would help identify where learning actually adds value under changing demand, connectivity, and degraded capability.

**Integrated validation on common mission/event scenarios.** Single-spacecraft continuous planning [111](https://ai.jpl.nasa.gov/public/projects/ipex/), four-satellite observation coordination [114](https://www.nasa.gov/smallspacecraft/what-is-starling/), and cross-operator maneuver coordination [115](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/ames/nasa-starling-and-spacex-starlink-improve-space-traffic-coordination/) each demonstrate a specific operational connection. What happens when task competition, network changes, and safety actions occur together remains a system-level evaluation question. Reproducible common event sequences could link asset states, workload, and disturbance records across simulation, software/hardware tests, and limited flight trials, making it possible to study where separately effective algorithms remain consistent and timely when combined.
