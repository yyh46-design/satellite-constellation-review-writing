# Mission-Oriented Orbital Design and Operational Decision-Making for Large Satellite Constellations: A Review

## Abstract

Orbital configurations and their evolution determine the spatial and temporal distribution of mission opportunities, while mission requirements, resource availability, and execution outcomes influence configuration choices and orbital actions. This review connects orbital design and dynamical modeling with deployment, station keeping, mission-driven reconfiguration, collision avoidance, and coordinated mission-resource planning. It examines the conditions for separate, hierarchical, and partially joint decision-making. The reviewed comparisons show that performance metrics can lead to different configuration choices: in the examined repeating-ground-track and regional-observation cases, equal coverage fractions correspond to different worst-case revisit times, and longer average coverage durations can accompany longer maximum observation gaps. Natural drift links deployment and reconfiguration, subject to available time and platform constraints. Resource bottlenecks change the mission value of orbital opportunities, and subsequent station-keeping requirements can change avoidance maneuvers. Common safety also requires compatible control inputs; access to all spacecraft states alone does not resolve conflicting actions. These findings support retaining feedback that changes feasibility or mission value and choosing the scope of coordinated decisions according to dependencies and response deadlines. Thousand-spacecraft configurations have been analyzed geometrically, whereas evidence combining orbital decisions, mission resources, and execution mainly addresses individual parts of the operational problem. Priorities include the long-term operational consequences of initial configurations, mission capability during staged deployment, and task continuation under finite control authority and uncertain states.

**Keywords:** large satellite constellations; orbital design; dynamical modeling; constellation reconfiguration; operational decision-making; mission-resource coordination
